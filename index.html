How the model works

1 · Rate every team

Each team gets a rating in points, fitted to the betting-market point spreads on every game that has one. Recent weeks count more (each older week weighs 0.7× the next). The starting quarterback is split out: each QB has a value from his efficiency per play (EPA) over recent seasons, and the market lines set how many points that is worth. A backup starting only lowers a team for the games he starts. A rating of +3 means a 3-point favorite over an average team on a neutral field. Home field is fitted too, currently … points.

2 · Turn ratings into win chances

A point gap becomes a win chance using a curve fitted to every game from 2002 to 2017 and checked on 2018 to 2025. A team favored by 3 points wins about 60% of the time, and a 7-point favorite about 73%. Games with a posted line use the market line directly.

3 · Play the season 10,000 times

Every remaining game is played at random using those chances. Each run also lets every team's strength drift up or down week by week, because injuries happen and teams change over a season. The drift is sized from how much betting-market ratings have actually moved in past seasons. Games with this week's lines get no drift, and games far off get the most (up to about … points by season's end). Tested by replaying 2006 to 2025 from Weeks 3, 8 and 12.

4 · Seed and play the playoffs

Each run builds the standings, breaks ties with the NFL's tiebreakers (head-to-head, division record, common games, conference record, strength of victory and schedule), seeds 7 teams per conference, and plays out every round. Run on the final standings of every season from 2002 to 2025, this seeding code reproduces all 48 real conference brackets exactly. Super Bowl LXI is at SoFi Stadium, so the Rams or Chargers get half the usual home-field edge if they reach it. The percentages are how often each outcome happened.

What it leaves out

The model can't know how long an injured starter will be out, so it assumes the latest starter keeps playing. Use the Starting QB menu in the team panel to test a return. Injuries at other positions count only once the betting market reacts to them. Tied games aren't simulated (they're under 1% of games). The rare points-based tiebreakers that come after strength of schedule are settled by coin flip, which happens in fewer than 1 in 1,000 simulated seasons.

Two ratings

The playoff odds use the market rating. The comparison section uses our own score-only rating, which lost to the market in every test season. It is there to show why the market makes the better foundation.

Updates

Scores and betting lines refresh automatically every morning from the public nflverse dataset. The page re-runs itself when new data arrives, and the refresh switches off after the last regular-season game.
